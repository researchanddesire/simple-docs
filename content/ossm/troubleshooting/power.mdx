If you can do it safely, unplug the power supply from the wall. Stop using your OSSM if you see sparks or smoke, smell burning, or the plug, the socket on the board or the power supply is scorched, melted or too hot to touch. Don't keep testing it. ## What your OSSM needs

- **24 V DC** into the barrel jack on the OSSM control board. The jack takes a 2.1 mm × 5.5 mm plug.
- **The power supply that came with your OSSM.** The R+D 24 V power supply is rated for 5 A continuous output.
- **Or the R+D USB-C adapter** with a USB-C PD charger that can supply 19 V at 65 W or more, and a USB-C cable. A lower-power charger may not run your OSSM at full performance.

<Callout type="warning" title="Other power supplies">

Third-party power supplies can damage the OSSM board or fail to give it enough power. Many supplies can't deliver their rated current continuously. Use a 24 V supply only: supplies of 30 V or more have destroyed parts on some OSSM boards.

</Callout>

## Check the power, step by step

1. Unplug the power supply from the wall.
2. Read the label on the power supply. The output should say **24 V** DC.
3. Check that the barrel plug is pushed all the way into the barrel jack on the control board.
4. Keep clear of the rail and plug the power supply back in. The OSSM starts homing straight away. The remote should show the start-up animation, and the light on the control board should breathe purple while the OSSM homes.
5. Check that the status light on the motor shows it has power.

## Nothing lights up

The remote screen stays blank and the light on the board stays off.

1. Try a different wall outlet.
2. Check both ends of the power supply: the cord into the supply and the barrel plug into the board.
3. Using the USB-C adapter? Check your charger can supply 19 V at 65 W or more, and try the 24 V power supply if you have it.
4. If the board light comes on but the remote screen stays blank, the remote or its cable is the likely cause. See [Wired Remote](/ossm/troubleshooting/wired-remote#the-screen-stays-blank).
5. Still nothing? [Contact support](#contact-support).

## The remote works, but the motor doesn't move

You see the start-up screens, the carriage doesn't move, and after about 40 seconds the screen shows **Homing took too long. Please check your wiring and try again.**

This usually means the control board has power but the motor doesn't. Check:

- **USB only.** A USB cable to a computer powers the board and remote, but not the motor. Plug in the 24 V power supply.
- **USB-C adapter.** A charger that can't supply 19 V at 65 W may not power the motor properly. Try the 24 V power supply.
- **Motor power wires.** With the power supply unplugged, check that the red 24V and black GND wires of the motor harness are fully seated, with no loose strands. Use the [wiring guide](https://ohai.researchanddesire.com/ossm/cable-harnesses).

If the motor's status light is on but the carriage still doesn't move, check the signal plugs: see [Check motor power and wiring](/ossm/troubleshooting/homing#check-motor-power-and-wiring). If the motor's status light stays off after these checks, [contact support](#contact-support).

## The OSSM restarts by itself

You can tell your OSSM has restarted when the remote shows the start-up animation again and the carriage measures the rail again, without you unplugging anything.

This can happen when the power supply can't keep up while the motor works hard, for example during play or when the carriage reaches the end of the rail while homing. A plug that loses contact can cause it too.

1. Use the R+D 24 V power supply, or a USB-C PD charger of 65 W or more with the adapter.
2. Check the barrel plug is pushed all the way in and doesn't move.
3. If it still restarts with the R+D power supply, [contact support](#contact-support) and say when it happens.

## Power cuts in and out when the plug moves

1. Unplug the power supply from the wall.
2. Look at the barrel plug and the socket on the board. If either is scorched, melted or bent, stop using the OSSM and [contact support](#contact-support).
3. Otherwise, push the plug in firmly and keep the cable from pulling on it.
4. If power still drops when the cable moves, [contact support](#contact-support). A short video of the plug moving and the power dropping helps.

## A pop or spark when you plug in

Stop and read the safety callout at the top of this page. Don't keep plugging and unplugging to test it.
